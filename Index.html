How to use this

Drawing with a mouse or touch

  • Click empty space to place an atom of the selected element.
  • Drag from an atom to grow a bond. Release over empty space for a new atom, or over an existing atom to connect them. Bond angles snap to 30° steps.
  • Click an existing atom to change it to the selected element.
  • Click a bond to apply the selected bond type. Clicking with Single selected cycles single → double → triple.
  • Switch to Move to reposition atoms, or Erase to delete.

What the checks mean

  • Valence. Bond orders are summed per atom and compared against the allowed valences for that element and formal charge. Period-2 atoms (C, N, O, F) can never exceed an octet; S, P, Se, and the heavier halogens may expand.
  • Lone pairs and octet. Derived, not assumed — so a nitrogen drawn with four bonds and no charge is flagged, which is the single most common structure-drawing error.
  • Unsaturation. Index of hydrogen deficiency, (2C + 2 + N − H − X)/2. Oxygen and sulfur are ignored; halogens count as hydrogens.
  • Aromaticity. Rings of 3–8 atoms are tested for a continuous p-orbital system, then counted against Hückel's 4n+2 rule. Rings holding 4n π electrons are called out as antiaromatic if planar.

Accuracy and limits

  • Wedge and hash bonds are recorded and drawn, but R/S descriptors are not assigned — check those yourself.
  • Structures are drawn flat. Ring conformation (chair, boat) is not modelled.
  • Resonance is not delocalised automatically; draw the contributor you want to discuss.
  • Monoisotopic masses use the most abundant isotope of each element and are for reference, not instrument calibration.
